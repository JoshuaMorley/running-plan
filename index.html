How to take gels

  • Take each gel with water, a few mouthfuls. Don't take a gel with sports drink as well, because the extra sugar can upset your stomach.
  • Open it a minute before the aid station or your km mark and take it over 20–30 seconds. Don't slow down to take it.
  • Build up in training: about 60 g per hour in the 29 km, then your race amount in the 33 km. Your gut adapts with practice.
  • Caffeine gels: PeakFuel Berry, Cola and Espresso have 30 mg each, Lime has none (check the packet). Use non-caffeine gels early, then caffeine ones from about km 18, when it does the most good. Keep the total to about 3–6 mg per kg of body weight, counting your morning coffee. Use the same mix in the 33 km first.
  • Carry your own. Check aucklandmarathon.co.nz for what is handed out on course, and don't rely on a brand you haven't tried.
  • Stomach trouble in training? Space gels out more and use more water. Don't give up on them. This is exactly what the practice runs are for.

Carb load · Fri 30 & Sat 31 Oct

  • Rice, pasta, bread, bagels, potatoes, pancakes, juice, lollies.
  • Cut back on fibre, fat and big salads, so your stomach feels light.
  • You'll put on 1–2 kg of water with the stored carbs. That's normal, and it's fuel.

Race breakfast

  • Something you've tested, such as white toast or a bagel with honey or jam, a banana, and juice.
  • About 500 ml of water or electrolyte drink with it, then sip until 30 min before the start.
  • Eat exactly the same breakfast before the 33 km, the half and the 13 km race practice.

How to use them

  • Everyday shoes for everything else: easy runs, the Tuesday 5ks, the 29 km and the shakeout. Those runs only wear the shoes down.
  • About 85 km before race day is enough to bed them in. Most race shoes are made to last a few hundred km, so there's plenty left for the marathon.
  • New pair? Run an easy 5–8 km in them before 6 Oct to check the fit, then follow the table.
  • Use the 33 km to sort out your kit: socks, how you lace them, and where you need tape or anti-chafe. Then don't change anything.
  • Sore calves or Achilles after the 33 km? Stiff, plated shoes load them harder. Wear everyday shoes for the next few days, then go back to the table.
  • Nothing new on race day: same shoes, socks and laces as the half and the 13 km.

Stay in one piece

Being fit won't help if you're injured on race day. These rules come before any session in the plan.

Every week

  1. Easy days are easy. HR under 150, and you can talk in full sentences. Easy days run too fast are the usual cause of breakdowns.
  2. Three hard days at most: Tuesday, Thursday and Sunday. If you race on Tuesday, keep Thursday controlled.
  3. Monday is always rest. It follows your biggest day of the week.
  4. After long runs and hard sessions, have carbs and protein within an hour, such as chocolate milk or a sandwich. Aim for 8 hours of sleep.
  5. Weekly volume goes up by no more than 10%. That's the limit in your NavRun profile, and the sub-3 build stays just inside it.

When something feels off

  1. Pain rule. Soreness that eases as you warm up is normal. Pain that gets worse, changes your stride, or stays sharp means stop, take 2 days off, and only come back when you can jog without pain.
  2. Tired signs: resting HR 5 or more beats above normal, heavy legs for 2 days or more, or poor sleep. Swap your next hard session for an easy one.
  3. Missed a session? Skip it. Never double up to make one up.
  4. Sick? Symptoms above the neck only: an easy run is OK. Fever, chest symptoms or aches: rest until you're clear.
  5. A test that doesn't go well is still useful. Switching routes is the plan doing its job.

Taper feelings are normal

From 19 Oct, volume drops and you may feel flat, sluggish or restless, with phantom niggles. That's your body absorbing the training. Don't add extra runs to feel better. The freshness shows up on race day.
